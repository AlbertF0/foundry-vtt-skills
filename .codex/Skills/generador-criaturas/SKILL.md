# Generador Criaturas

Use this skill to turn a creature, NPC, monster, or player-character brief into an image-generation prompt for a consistent campaign portrait style.

The current campaign style is **refined gothic art nouveau fantasy portraiture** with **Chapter 0 portrait continuity**: elegant, readable, slightly stylized character art with dense narrative backgrounds, fine ink linework, and layered watercolor/gouache tonal shading. It should feel like a serious illustrated fantasy sourcebook crossed with gothic civic architecture and restrained art nouveau portraiture, not like generic concept art, photorealism, anime, 3D render, monster splash art, gritty comic art, or a decorative poster.

The portraits must have a **strong illustrated identity**. They should never drift into realistic portrait painting, cinematic realism, lifelike skin rendering, photographic lighting, or 3D concept art. At the same time, they must not become cartoon, chibi, comic caricature, children's-book simplification, anime, or flat vector mascot art. Aim for a mature hand-illustrated fantasy plate: stylized but serious, expressive but not exaggerated, graphic and characterful but anatomically readable.

## Campaign Reference Look

Use the Chapter 0 portrait set as the visual anchor: Alessandra, Evander, Hannel, and Jerot Galgin. New portraits should feel like they were painted for the same book and cropped for the same Foundry collection.

The reference look is:

- close portrait framing with the character large in the image, usually from mid-thigh, waist, or bust upward; hands and signature props are often visible in the lower half;
- elegant but natural facial proportions, with expressive eyes, carefully drawn noses, mouths, brows, and hands; attractive or severe faces may be painterly, but must remain visibly illustrated;
- fine black and dark-brown ink linework throughout the image, including facial features, hair strands, clothing folds, jewelry, hands, props, and background architecture;
- clean layered watercolor, gouache, or ink-wash shading over clean linework, with smooth tonal planes and very selective material texture only where needed; not flat poster color, not glossy digital render, and not paper grain or noisy grit across the whole image;
- dense gothic architectural interiors or ship interiors with arched windows, ribbed vaults, mullions, lanterns, candles, carved panels, banners, masks, bones, books, tables, tools, or other narrative props;
- backgrounds drawn with real linework and detail, not blurred ambience, but kept slightly darker, cooler, and lower contrast than the character;
- subdued blue-gray, charcoal, bone ivory, black, muted brass, wine red, dark wood, cold stone, and candle amber palettes, with occasional sky blue or magical color accents;
- dramatic but controlled value contrast: the face and hands read first, then clothing and signature prop, then background;
- integrated ornament in clothing, architecture, jewelry, symbols, lanterns, windows, masks, staffs, bones, and tools. Ornament should look functional or symbolic, not like a separate border.

Prompt for this look explicitly with phrases such as **fine ink-and-watercolor gothic art nouveau character illustration**, **detailed dark linework**, **clean watercolor/gouache tonal shading**, **smooth controlled value planes**, **dense gothic architectural background**, **serious sourcebook portrait**, and **Chapter 0 campaign portrait continuity**.

Avoid outputs that look too smooth, too simplified, too airbrushed, too bright, too high-fantasy-splash, too painterly without linework, too flat-vector, too empty in the background, or too dirty/grainy/noisy. Treat **global texture noise as a hard failure**: no speckled pigment, no sandy paper surface, no gritty digital overlay, no stippled dirt, no random scratches, and no micro-hatching spread across the whole image. Detail must come from deliberate ink linework and clean value shapes, not from noise.

## Hard Style Lock

The style must read as **Art Nouveau first** and dark fantasy second.

The image must read as **illustration, not realism**. Every prompt should actively reinforce:

- visible hand-inked contour lines and designed shape language;
- simplified, elegant planes of color rather than lifelike pores, realistic skin texture, or cinematic material rendering;
- expressive but restrained faces with character, not photo likeness and not cartoon exaggeration;
- stylized anatomy with believable proportions, not hyperreal anatomy, uncanny realism, chibi proportions, or mascot simplification;
- decorative rhythm integrated into real clothing, hair, props, and background shapes.

Always prioritize:

- close readable portrait framing with the character occupying most of the vertical frame;
- fine black and dark-brown ink lines over clean watercolor/gouache tonal shading with smooth controlled value planes;
- detailed faces, hands, hair, clothing folds, and signature props in the same visual family as the Chapter 0 portraits;
- dense but disciplined gothic or spelljammer architecture behind the character, drawn with real linework rather than blurred atmosphere;
- graceful vertical composition with elegant negative space;
- long sinuous S-curves in hair, cloaks, tails, smoke, wings, tentacles, horns, banners, arches, windows, branches, water, or magical residue;
- clean dark contour lines with varied weight, closer to refined ink illustration than rough comic inking;
- broad clean color shapes with subtle painterly modeling, not all-over grain or speckled texture; use smooth calm wash planes and only a few deliberate material marks where they describe bark, stone, metal, cloth, skin, scale, or shell;
- intentional stylization: graphic silhouettes, designed facial planes, selective detail, and a clear editorial fantasy illustration finish;
- backgrounds that make sense for the specific creature, role, plane, habitat, or original reference image; use architecture only when it belongs to that creature's scene;
- elegant restraint, symmetry or graceful asymmetry, and readable anatomy.

Strongly avoid:

- photorealism, realistic portraiture, cinematic realism, lifelike skin, pore detail, subsurface skin rendering, realistic camera depth of field, photographic lens effects, 3D render materials, glossy video-game concept art, gritty comic-book rendering, scratchy texture, noisy hatching, paper grain, speckled overlays, all-over dirt, dirty wash over skin or clothing, chaotic brushwork, splash-art poses, action-scene energy, extreme snarling closeups, excessive gore, chaotic flames, heavy smoke clouds, random magical glow, cluttered props, and over-rendered concept-art lighting.

For every generated monster or NPC prompt, explicitly include: **clean smooth ink-and-gouache finish, no global texture, no paper grain, no speckled noise, no gritty overlay, no noisy hatching, no dirt filter, no scratchy wash, no random micro-detail.** If the subject needs texture, describe it as intentional large readable forms: clean bark grooves, clean stone cracks, clean shell plate seams, clean cloth folds, clean scale outlines, clean gel highlights.

Also avoid drifting too far the other way:

- cartoon, chibi, anime, Disney-like stylization, Pixar-like 3D, mascot design, flat vector art, childish proportions, oversized heads, simplified dot eyes, rubbery expressions, slapstick poses, and caricatured anatomy.

For monsters, do not make them generic action creatures. Preserve the anatomy and the creature-appropriate setting, but redesign the image as a poised art nouveau bestiary portrait: dangerous, solemn, elegant, and clearly staged.

Monster portraits must still match the **same visual family as the NPC portraits**. Do not switch into a separate monster-manual, splash-art, horror-cover, or action-bestiary style. Treat monsters as elegant character plates: a clear full illustrated figure, refined linework, restrained pose energy, readable silhouette, polished gothic architecture or habitat, and the same muted stained-glass palette used for humanoid NPCs. Even when the creature is attacking, the image should feel like a composed portrait plate rather than a frozen combat scene.

## Background Fidelity Rule

Never force every subject into a gothic hall, cathedral, crypt, or city interior.

If the user provides a source image, preserve the **type of background** unless they explicitly ask to change it. The exact composition can be cleaned, simplified, and restaged, but the setting category must remain coherent:

- astral creature -> astral void, spelljammer sky, floating stones, cosmic horizon, or planar rift;
- demon -> infernal cavern, abyssal ruin, volcanic plain, burnt battlefield, or fiendish lair;
- grave undead -> cemetery, graveyard, tomb exterior, crypt, catacomb, moonlit burial ground;
- arcane aberration -> ruined laboratory, wizard dungeon, rune chamber, sewer, underground archive, or cursed study;
- aquatic creature -> flooded ruin, underwater hall, river cave, canal, cistern, grotto, or elemental water vortex;
- ship/astral sailor -> ship deck, dock, tavern, chart room, mast rigging, lanterns, ropes, harbor, or astral sea;
- musician or performer -> haunted ballroom, tavern stage, ritual chamber, procession, crypt hall, or spectral salon;
- soldier/wraith -> battlefield ruin, old armory, city gate, barracks, crypt guard post, or cursed road.

The background should be narrative and readable, but not necessarily gothic. Art Nouveau should shape the lines, curves, silhouettes, color fields, and composition; it must not overwrite the creature's habitat.

## Workflow

1. Extract the subject: name, species/ancestry, role, age, build, expression, pose, clothing/armor, equipment, symbols, mood, and likely location.
2. Preserve user-specified canon details. Do not edit or overwrite source descriptions unless the user explicitly asks for file edits.
3. Write the image prompt in English unless the user asks otherwise; image models usually follow detailed English visual direction better.
4. Structure the prompt as: subject and composition, character details, background, style contract, palette and lighting, negative constraints.
5. Invent coherent fantasy details only when useful for readability or role identity. Keep additions specific and restrained.
6. Make the character readable at portrait size: face/head, hands, weapon or tool, faction mark, silhouette, and role-defining clothing should be clear.
7. Keep the background narrative and architectural, but lower contrast than the character.

## Required Format

Always generate a vertical portrait matching campaign-card proportions:

- Portrait orientation, full illustrated frame, 2:3 vertical aspect ratio such as 1024x1536.
- Bust-up, waist-up, or three-quarter portrait depending on the subject; avoid tiny full-body figures unless requested.
- The subject must remain centered or slightly asymmetrically centered, with a clear readable silhouette.
- Leave comfortable headroom above the head, horns, hat, crown, hair, or tallest silhouette detail. The head must never touch or nearly touch the top edge. Keep enough upper margin for later circular token cropping in Foundry VTT.
- Do not use square, horizontal, panoramic, circular token crop, transparent isolated background, cropped head-only portrait, UI card layout, or text labels unless explicitly requested.

## Output Naming

When generating portraits from description files, save each final image as a `.webp` file using the character name as the filename slug. If the source file follows the pattern `[name]-descripcion.txt`, remove the `-descripcion` suffix and save the image as `[name].webp` in the requested output folder, for example `lupin-descripcion.txt` -> `lupin.webp`. Keep filenames lowercase, hyphenated, and stable so Foundry VTT references remain predictable.

The image must read clearly as hand-drawn illustration: dense fine black and dark-brown linework over clean watercolor/gouache tonal shading, crisp facial features, carefully drawn hands, detailed hair, layered clothing folds, and meaningful props. Use graceful Art Nouveau rhythm in the pose, hair, cloak, smoke, banners, windows, arches, lanterns, roots, tails, tentacles, horns, or other real scene elements, but keep the anatomy and expression natural, mature, and characterful.

## Defining Visual Traits

Prioritize elegance and readability over busy decoration:

- Use graceful vertical composition, quiet rhythm, elegant negative space, and lightly elongated silhouettes.
- Keep the character large in the frame, similar to the Chapter 0 references; avoid distant full-body views unless the creature's anatomy requires it.
- Use dense fine linework and visible drawn detail across the whole image, not only the face. Backgrounds should be drawn, not implied by soft blur.
- Use clean layered watercolor/gouache value planes with minimal selective material texture; avoid smooth plastic airbrushed gradients, glossy digital polish, paper grain, speckled overlays, and dirty grunge washes.
- Let art nouveau influence the character pose, cloak flow, hair, smoke, banners, window shapes, arches, and negative space.
- Keep decoration integrated into real objects: tabards, clasps, weapons, crowns, armor trim, architectural arches, lantern brackets, window mullions, sarcophagus shapes, gate towers, and civic emblems.
- Avoid separate ornamental frames, border filigree, corner decorations, lace patterns, floating scrollwork, or decorative motifs that do not belong to the scene. Nouveau curves must be integrated into anatomy, clothing, props, architecture, smoke, water, hair, wings, tails, or environmental shapes.
- Use crisp outlines and clean shape design so the subject reads immediately.
- Use subtle painterly shading and selective texture only on appropriate materials; do not make the output glossy, photoreal, gritty, scratchy, noisy, speckled, dirty, comic-book-like, or over-rendered.
- Keep skin, scales, fur, cloth, metal, and stone stylized. Use clean illustrated planes, selective accents, and linework instead of realistic pores, hyper-detailed fibers, photographic reflections, all-over grain, dirty overlays, or 3D material shaders.
- Give every subject clear personality through posture, silhouette, face shape, expression, clothing rhythm, and focal props. The character should feel specific and memorable without becoming a caricature.
- Keep faces, hands, weapons, symbols, armor, jewelry, crowns, and faction emblems especially sharp.

## Background Rule

Always include a real narrative background that fits the character. The style works best when the background is architectural and readable, not blank or abstract.

Good background types:

- Old fantasy city streets, gatehouses, watch posts, narrow alleys, civic squares, bridges, towers, guard barracks, stone stairways, markets, taverns, temples, archives, libraries, crypts, tomb corridors, castle halls, throne rooms, cloisters, laboratories, chapel-like interiors, astral ship decks, planar voids, infernal caverns, flooded ruins, graveyards, battlefield ruins, sewers, grottoes, and creature-specific lairs.

For mundane or civic characters:

- Prefer daylight, overcast daylight, morning patrol light, lantern-lit evening, or cool city shade.
- Use pale limestone, slate shadows, oxidized green, dark teal cloth, brass accents, iron gray, aged ivory, and muted warm stone.
- Make the setting grounded: pointed doorways, arched windows, clean cobblestone shapes, lantern brackets, gate towers, hanging cloth signs without text, rooftops, banners, guard posts, or city emblems.

For undead, occult, noble, or monstrous characters:

- Use the setting that fits the creature first. Crypts, gothic halls, chapel ruins, tomb niches, sarcophagi, throne rooms, candlelit stairs, old banners, or shadowed arches are appropriate for many undead and occult figures, but not for every monster.
- Keep the scene structured and readable, with darkness used as value contrast rather than as an excuse to hide the subject.

Background rendering:

- Lower contrast than the character.
- Clear architecture with simplified shapes.
- Quiet vertical rhythm from arches, windows, columns, towers, niches, or banners.
- Subtle curves may appear in arch lines, smoke, cloak movement, window shapes, or banners.
- Do not use an empty studio backdrop unless explicitly requested.

## Linework And Definition

The style needs enough outline to read clearly:

- Use a visible dark outer contour around the main silhouette, head, shoulders, hands, important clothing edges, weapons, and key props.
- Vary line weight by importance: medium-thin and elegant on outer contours, finer on interior detail.
- Add fine interior ink lines and controlled cross-contour detail like the reference portraits: facial planes, eyelids, lips, knuckles, hair locks, cloth wrinkles, seams, jewelry, staff carving, armor edges, and background mullions should be visibly drawn.
- Make the strongest edges on the face/head, hands, focal prop, and role-defining equipment.
- Use clean linework for anatomy, clothing seams, armor plates, weapon edges, jewelry, emblems, and facial features.
- Use minimal controlled hatching only if it helps form; do not let hatching dominate.
- Avoid thick cartoon outlines, rough sketch lines, messy linework, weak outlines, and overly soft undefined faces.
- Avoid realistic portrait rendering where linework disappears into soft painterly skin. The face should be expressive and dimensional, but still visibly illustrated with designed planes, crisp feature lines, and controlled stylization.

## Color And Palette

Use rich but restrained fantasy colors. The palette should feel like the Chapter 0 references: dark gothic interiors, cool blue-gray and charcoal shadows, bone ivory highlights, muted brass, dark wood, wine red, cold stone, and small candle-amber or magical accents. It should feel gothic, civic, and elegant, not neon, sepia, or one-note.

Core palette families:

- Dark teal, oxidized green, slate blue, charcoal black, cold iron gray.
- Aged ivory, pale limestone, muted warm stone, bone ivory.
- Deep plum, desaturated violet-black, smoky black, Payne's gray, indigo shadow.
- Muted brass, antique gold, old bronze, small crimson or garnet accents.
- Lantern amber or candlelight may be used as a small warm accent, not a magical bloom unless requested.

Use daylight when it fits the character:

- Daylight should be pale, cool, and readable: soft slate-blue shadows, pale stone walls, restrained warm highlights, clear value separation.
- Day scenes should remain gothic and elegant, not cheerful high-saturation fantasy.

Use night or interior light when it fits:

- Night scenes should retain readable silhouettes and architecture.
- Avoid excessive darkness that hides anatomy, weapons, clothing, or expression.

## Character Adaptation

Adapt the style to the role without forcing every subject to look supernatural.

For city guards, soldiers, mercenaries, captains, and civic NPCs:

- Ground them in believable practical gear: leather-and-mail armor, tabards, helmets, cloaks, sword belts, polearms, keys, whistles, lanterns, badges, city emblems, worn gloves.
- Use disciplined, watchful, slightly weary poses rather than exaggerated hero stances.
- Favor old city streets, gatehouses, watch posts, or barracks backgrounds.
- Keep ornament modest: brass emblem, shaped tabard, cloak curve, arched doorway, lantern bracket.

For nobles, vampires, liches, undead kings, cultists, and occult figures:

- Use more elongated shapes, high collars, crowns, jewelry, ceremonial robes, bone or ivory accents, candles, crypts, arches, tomb niches, or chapel architecture.
- Keep menace solemn, regal, cursed, mournful, or haunted rather than gory.
- Crowns, masks, holy symbols, books, relics, and hands should be crisp focal points.

For beasts and monsters:

- Preserve creature anatomy clearly through silhouette and line.
- Use art nouveau curves in horns, tails, wings, fur masses, tendrils, smoke, or surrounding architecture.
- Avoid turning monsters into abstract decorations; the body must remain readable.
- Match the NPC portrait finish: clean elegant outlines, refined painted planes, calm architectural staging, restrained palette, and a poised readable figure. Avoid making monsters harsher, more realistic, more game-concept-like, or more action-heavy than the NPC portraits.
- Prefer a composed three-quarter or full-figure portrait stance over extreme lunges, cropped attacks, Dutch angles, explosive motion, or foreground claws unless the user description absolutely requires that exact action.

## Clean Detail Rule

Age, wear, dirt, ruin, and darkness should be readable design details, not noisy texture.

Prefer:

- Clean chips on stone, a few cracks, worn edges, simple stains, controlled shadow shapes, deliberate scars, faded cloth patches, patinated metal, readable grime only in small areas.
- Smooth clean skin, cloth, fur, scales, and metal value planes with linework doing most of the detail.
- Background texture as drawn objects and lines: cracks, seams, carvings, mullions, wood grain, map lines, candle wax, and metal edges, not a global dirty overlay.

Avoid:

- All-over dirt, gritty overlays, dusty haze, paper grain, speckled noise, global noise filters, muddy texture, excessive scratches, corroded micro-detail, dirty pores, rubble noise, dirty wash over skin or clothing, or texture for texture's sake.

## Quick Quality Check

Before finalizing a prompt, confirm:

- The image is a vertical 2:3 portrait.
- There is comfortable headroom above the head, horns, hat, crown, hair, or tallest silhouette detail, with enough margin for later circular token cropping.
- The image matches the Chapter 0 campaign reference look: fine ink-and-watercolor/gouache gothic art nouveau illustration, not generic concept art.
- The image is clean: smooth controlled watercolor/gouache value planes, no paper grain, no speckled noise, no dirty overlay, no all-over grit.
- The subject is large in the frame, usually bust-up, waist-up, mid-thigh, or close three-quarter rather than distant full-body.
- The subject has a clear role, silhouette, expression, and focal details.
- The face/head, hands, and important props are explicitly crisp.
- The background is a real narrative place, not blank or abstract.
- The background is detailed with real linework, readable, and lower contrast than the character; it is not blurred ambience.
- The style includes gothic art nouveau traits: elegant linework, natural but slightly stylized proportions, restrained flowing curves, layered ink-wash color and watercolor/gouache shading.
- There is no decorative frame, filigree border, ornate corner flourish, or excessive scrollwork.
- The palette is restrained and varied, with gothic civic colors and small warm metallic accents.
- Lighting matches the role and remains readable, whether daylight, evening, crypt, or interior.
- Negative constraints block photorealism, realistic portraiture, cinematic lighting, 3D, anime, cartoon simplification, chibi/mascot/caricature drift, glossy video-game splash art, modern clothing, text, empty backgrounds, and messy clutter.
